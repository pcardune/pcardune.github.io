Kelvin, bar and g

Plain meanings for the units used for temperature, pressure and gravity.

Three units turn up all the time when talking about worlds.

Kelvin (K) is a temperature scale that starts at absolute zero, the coldest possible temperature, where atoms barely move. The steps are the same size as Celsius degrees, so a kelvin is the same size as a degree Celsius. Add 273 to a Celsius temperature to get kelvin. Water freezes at 273 K and boils at 373 K. The Sun's surface is about 5,800 K. Scientists like kelvin because it never goes negative, and it makes the physics simpler.

Bar is a unit of pressure, and it is close to the air pressure at Earth's sea level, which is about 1.01 bar. A millibar (mbar) is a thousandth of that. Mars's air is about 6 mbar. Venus's is about 92 bar (see Air pressure).

g is gravity compared to Earth's. A world with 1 g pulls as hard as Earth does. Mars is about 0.4 g, the Moon about 0.17 g, and Jupiter's cloud tops about 2.5 g (see Gravity).

In Space Time, a world's temperature is mainly shown in degrees Celsius, and its star's in kelvin.
