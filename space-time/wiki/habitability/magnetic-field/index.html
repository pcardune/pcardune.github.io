Magnetic field

The invisible shield that helps a planet keep its air.

A planet's magnetic field comes from liquid metal churning in its core. It reaches far out into space and steers the solar wind, the stream of charged particles flowing from the star, around the planet.

That matters because a planet with no shield can have its air stripped away, a little at a time. Mars lost its field billions of years ago, and its atmosphere is now very thin. A field also blocks particles that damage living cells, though a thick atmosphere can do part of that job too, which is how Venus gets by without one.

Making a field takes a molten core and a fast enough spin. Small worlds cool and freeze solid, and slow rotators struggle.

In Space Time, gas and ice giants always have strong fields, dwarf planets have none, and slowly rotating worlds, including tidally locked ones, end up with a weak field or none.
