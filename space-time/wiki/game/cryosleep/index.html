Cryosleep

How the crew sleeps through the years while a world changes below.

Cryosleep is science fiction. Nobody has ever been frozen and woken again, though some animals hibernate and doctors sometimes cool patients to protect them. In stories, a crew goes into deep sleep to skip the long, empty stretches of a journey. Space Time borrows the idea for a different job.

Worlds change over thousands of years, far longer than anyone could wait. So when the ship is in orbit, the crew can go to sleep and galaxy time speeds up to ten, a hundred or a thousand times its usual pace. The ship's instruments keep recording while the crew sleeps, so the readings fill in like a time-lapse of the world below: air thinning, oceans freezing over, life spreading.

The crew doesn't sleep through everything. They wake when something needs attention, such as a reading going out of its safe range in the system, or a condition they set beforehand comes true, like a gas reaching a chosen level.

Cryosleep is only possible in orbit, a safe place to leave the ship. A fast sleep can overshoot a wake condition a little, since the readings are checked a few times a second.
