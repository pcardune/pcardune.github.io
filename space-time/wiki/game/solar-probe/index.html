Solar probe

The small scout the ship launches to find new star systems.

A solar probe is a small, uncrewed scout. Real probes such as Voyager and Cassini explored the solar system without a crew, and many of them borrowed speed and direction from the gravity of the planets they passed.

In Space Time, the ship can see stars from afar but can't see their planets. A probe that flies into a star system discovers it, and the system's planets appear. One probe discovers every unexplored system it passes through, so a single launch can chart several.

Gravity does the steering. A probe is pulled by every star near its path, and heavier stars pull harder, so its route bends as it flies. The curved line the ship shows when aiming is a preview of that bend.

Probes are short-lived. They are lost if they fly into a star or run out of time. The ship carries a limited supply, and each planet it surveys restocks one.

When a probe's flight ends, the log says how many systems it found.
