Wiki / Stars

M dwarf

The small, dim, red stars that make up most of the galaxy.

M dwarfs, or red dwarfs, are the smallest, coolest and dimmest of the common stars, with surfaces below about 3,700 K that glow orange-red. They are also the most common kind, roughly three in four stars in our galaxy. They burn their fuel so slowly that they can shine for trillions of years.

They are hard on their planets. Their habitable zones are very close in, so planets there are often tidally locked. And many red dwarfs flare violently, especially when young, blasting their planets with particles and ultraviolet light that can strip away air. A strong magnetic field helps.

Proxima Centauri, the nearest star to the Sun, is a red dwarf with a planet in its habitable zone. TRAPPIST-1, about 40 light-years away, has seven Earth-sized planets.

In Space Time, red dwarfs are the most common stars too, but not three in four, so you meet more variety. Worlds around them are more often stripped of their air.
