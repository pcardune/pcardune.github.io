Wiki / Stars

Blue stars

O and B stars, the hottest, brightest and shortest-lived.

O stars and B stars are the giants of the star world. Their surfaces run from about 10,000 K up past 30,000 K, which makes them blue-white, and they are many times the Sun's mass. Rigel and Spica are B stars. O stars are rarer still.

They are enormously bright. An O star can outshine the Sun by hundreds of thousands of times. They pay for that by burning out fast: an O star lives only a few million years, which is far too short for life to get going. They end in a supernova, a titanic explosion that can leave a black hole or a neutron star.

Their light is mostly ultraviolet, so any planet nearby is bathed in radiation that sterilizes the surface and strips air. They also blow a powerful stellar wind (see Stellar wind and flares).

Blue stars rarely have habitable worlds, but they light up the nebulae where new stars are born.
