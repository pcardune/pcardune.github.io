Dwarf planet

A small, round world that has not cleared its path, like Pluto and Ceres.

A dwarf planet is a world that orbits a star and is big enough for its own gravity to pull it round, but that has not cleared other objects out of its path. Pluto was moved into the group in 2006. Ceres in the asteroid belt, Eris and Makemake are others.

Most dwarf planets are small and cold. Pluto is smaller than our Moon. Yet they can have surprising features. Pluto has a huge heart-shaped plain of frozen nitrogen that moves like a slow glacier, mountains of water ice, and a thin haze of blue-tinted air. Ceres has bright salt deposits that come from a briny layer underneath.

Their small size means they have weak gravity and little internal heat, so most are geologically dead and covered in craters. They can hold only a thin atmosphere, or none.

The label depends on a world's size and surroundings, so there is no sharp line between a small dwarf planet and a large moon or asteroid.

In Space Time, dwarf planets have no magnetic field and are small and cratered. A few are kept active by tidal heating.
