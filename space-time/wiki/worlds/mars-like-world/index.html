Wiki / Worlds

Mars-like world

A small, cold planet with thin air, which once may have been wetter.

Mars is about half Earth's width and has just over a third of its gravity. Its atmosphere is mostly carbon dioxide, but less than one percent as thick as Earth's. It is cold, averaging about -60 °C, and dusty, with huge dust storms. The ground is red from iron oxide, rust. It has polar caps of water ice, topped in winter by frozen carbon dioxide, and the largest volcano in the solar system, Olympus Mons.

Mars used to be different. Dry riverbeds, lake basins and minerals formed in water show that liquid water flowed on its surface billions of years ago. The planet probably had a thicker atmosphere and a magnetic field then. As the planet cooled and its field died, the solar wind stripped much of its air away (see Magnetic field and Atmospheric escape), and the surface dried and froze.

A Mars-like world is a small, cold planet with a thin carbon dioxide atmosphere. Because its gravity is weak, it is hard for such a world to hold on to any air it is given.

Mars is also the main target for the idea of terraforming, making a planet more Earth-like on purpose.

In Space Time, small, cold worlds with thin carbon dioxide air are one of the starting types of planet.
