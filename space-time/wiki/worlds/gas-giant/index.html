Gas giant

A huge planet of hydrogen and helium with no surface to stand on.

A gas giant is a huge planet made mostly of hydrogen and helium, like Jupiter and Saturn. It has no solid surface. Its gas just gets denser and hotter with depth, turning to a liquid and perhaps a small core deep inside. Jupiter is so big that more than 1,300 Earths could fit inside it.

The colorful bands are belts of clouds stretched out by the planet's fast spin. Giant storms sit among them. Jupiter's Great Red Spot is a storm wider than Earth that has been observed for centuries. Many gas giants have rings, and most have many moons.

Gas giants form beyond the frost line, where there is enough ice to build a large core that can pull in a lot of gas. They often have strong magnetic fields.

Some gas giants don't stay where they formed. A hot Jupiter is a gas giant that moved in so close to its star that its year lasts only a few days. The first ever found around a Sun-like star, 51 Pegasi b in 1995, was one. Their clouds can glow, and their temperatures can reach thousands of degrees.

In Space Time, giants have no climate to change, but their hydrogen can be collected, and the ship's fuel comes from it.
