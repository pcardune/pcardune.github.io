Steam world

A planet so hot that its oceans have boiled into the air.

A steam world is a planet that was warm enough for its oceans to boil away into a thick atmosphere of water vapor. The vapor is itself a powerful greenhouse gas, so once the process starts it feeds on itself (see Runaway greenhouse and Water vapor feedback). The planet gets hotter, more water boils off, and the air gets thicker, with surface pressure hundreds of times Earth's.

It is thought that Venus went this way. Early on it may have had oceans, and as the Sun brightened it heated past the point of no return. Then ultraviolet light split the water vapor high up, the light hydrogen leaked into space, and the water was lost for good (see Atmospheric escape). Venus is the dry result.

A steam world can be a stage in a world's life rather than its end. A very young planet is hot from its formation, and its first ocean may spend a while as steam until it cools enough for the water to rain out. Earth is thought to have had such an early phase.

Astronomers have also found hot rocky exoplanets that could be steam worlds, but the air is hard to measure.
