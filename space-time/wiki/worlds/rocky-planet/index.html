Rocky planet

A world with a solid surface of rock and metal, like Earth, Mars and Venus.

A rocky planet, also called a terrestrial planet, is a world with a solid surface made mostly of rock and metal. In our own solar system there are four: Mercury, Venus, Earth and Mars. They are small compared with the giant planets, and they have a dense metal core under a rocky mantle and crust.

Rocky planets form close to their star, inside the frost line, where only rock and metal can be solid. Whether they hold an atmosphere is another matter, and it varies enormously. Mercury has almost none, Mars has a thin one, Earth has a moderate one, and Venus has a crushing one.

They are also the worlds where life is most likely, because they have a surface to hold oceans and chemistry. Astronomers have found many rocky planets around other stars. Some are bigger than Earth and are called super-Earths.

What happens inside matters too. A rocky world's core, mantle and volcanoes shape its air and its magnetic field (see Tectonics and volcanism).

In Space Time, rocky planets mostly form close to their star, and only rocky planets, dwarf planets and moons have a climate you can change.
