Icy moons

Moons of ice with hidden oceans, among the best places to look for life beyond Earth.

An icy moon has a crust of water ice over a rocky core, and in many cases an ocean in between. They are common around the cold giant planets. Jupiter's Europa and Ganymede and Saturn's Enceladus and Titan all fit.

The surprise is the water. Far from the Sun, ice should stay frozen all the way through. But the gravity of a giant planet squeezes and flexes a nearby moon, and the friction heats it from inside (see Tidal heating). That keeps a deep ocean liquid under the ice, even where the surface is frozen solid at -150 °C or colder.

Enceladus gives the best proof. The Cassini spacecraft flew through geysers of water spraying from its south pole and found salt, organic molecules and signs of hot water inside. Europa has more water than all Earth's oceans put together.

That makes icy moons some of the most promising places to look for life beyond Earth. They have liquid water, minerals and heat, but no sunlight, so any life would have to live in the dark.
