Runaway greenhouse

How a planet can heat up until its oceans boil away.

In a runaway greenhouse, warming leads to more warming without limit, until a planet's oceans are gone. On a normal world, a warmer climate adds some water vapor to the air, which is a greenhouse gas that warms things further (see Water vapor feedback). Usually the effect settles. If a planet gets too much light, it doesn't.

Beyond a certain amount of sunlight, the planet can't shed heat as fast as it takes it in, and the oceans start to boil into the air. The vapor traps more heat, which evaporates more water, and so on until the ocean is steam (see Steam world). There is a threshold, and a planet that passes it can't turn back by cooling a little.

At the end, ultraviolet light breaks the vapor into hydrogen and oxygen high up, and the hydrogen escapes (see Atmospheric escape). The planet loses its water for good, which is one proposed history of Venus (see Venus-like world).

This sets the inner edge of a star's habitable zone. The Sun is slowly brightening, and in about a billion years it should be bright enough to push Earth over the edge.

In Space Time, a runaway greenhouse is one of the outcomes a world can head toward, and oceans boiling off is one of the warnings.
